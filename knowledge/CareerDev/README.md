Career Development at MetaMinds is not resume-writing — it is a program to help students discover what they want to do with their lives, build the skills that make them competitive, and take concrete steps during high school and college to get there.

This subject is taught by Graduate Mentors who have recently navigated the college-to-career transition and can speak authentically about what worked.

## Graduate Mentor Notes

Graduate Mentors teaching this subject should:

1. **Share their own story** — Students need to hear from people who recently lived this
2. **Be honest about failures** — What didn't work, what you'd do differently
3. **Connect to the student's specific goals** — Don't deliver a generic curriculum; customize
4. **Bring real documents** — Share your actual resume (redacted), your college essays (if comfortable), your Leetcode setup
5. **Make it action-oriented** — Every session should end with 1–2 concrete steps the student will take

| Grade | Top 3 Career Dev Priorities |
|-------|---------------------------|
| 9 | Find 1–2 passions, start coding, join 1 competition |
| 10 | Build GitHub, try a hackathon, start an EC leadership role |
| 11 | Start college research, take AP exams, do your first internship search |
| 12 | College apps, scholarship apps, senior project |
| College 1–2 | Career fairs, internship applications, Leetcode |
| College 3–4 | Return offers, grad school vs. work decision |

## Common Student Struggles

1. **Procrastination on applications** — Deadlines feel far away until they're not
2. **Not knowing what they want to do** — This is normal; focus on exploration, not commitment
3. **Resume too long or too short** — High schoolers often list everything; college students often leave out key projects
4. **Technical interview anxiety** — Leetcode can feel overwhelming; start with Easy problems
5. **Undervaluing soft skills** — STEM students think only technical skills matter; they're wrong
