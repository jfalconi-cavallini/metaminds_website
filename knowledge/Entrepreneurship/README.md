MetaMinds Entrepreneurship teaches students to identify problems, build solutions, and communicate their ideas. It is not a business administration course — it is a mindset course. Students learn to think like founders: to observe the world, find friction, and ask "what if we built something for this?"

This subject is especially relevant to MetaMinds students because:
- Many will build technical products (apps, robotics, tools)
- The Junior Mentor program itself is an entrepreneurial endeavor
- The skills — pitching, prototyping, validating — apply to every career

## Module Structure

### Module 1: Entrepreneurial Mindset
**Duration:** 2–3 sessions

Lessons:
1. What Is Entrepreneurship? Beyond "Starting a Business" (difficulty: 1)
2. Problem-Finding: Observing Friction in Daily Life (difficulty: 2)
3. Design Thinking Overview: Empathize, Define, Ideate, Prototype, Test (difficulty: 2)
4. Famous Founders & What They Actually Did (difficulty: 1)
5. Failure as Data: Iteration vs. Perfectionism (difficulty: 2)
6. The Mindset of a Builder (difficulty: 2)

---

### Module 2: Finding & Validating Problems
**Duration:** 2–3 sessions

Lessons:
1. Problem Statements: How to Write One (difficulty: 2)
2. Customer Discovery: Talking to People (difficulty: 3)
3. Who Is Your User? Empathy Mapping (difficulty: 2)
4. Market Sizing (TAM/SAM/SOM) Introduction (difficulty: 3)
5. Validating Assumptions: How to Know If a Problem Is Real (difficulty: 3)
6. Common Founder Mistakes: Building What No One Wants (difficulty: 2)

---

### Module 3: Ideation & Prototyping
**Duration:** 2–3 sessions

Lessons:
1. Brainstorming Techniques: SCAMPER, Mind Mapping (difficulty: 2)
2. How to Select an Idea: Feasibility vs. Desirability (difficulty: 3)
3. Minimum Viable Product (MVP): What Is the Minimum? (difficulty: 3)
4. Paper Prototyping (difficulty: 2)
5. Digital Prototyping: Figma Basics (difficulty: 3)
6. Getting Feedback Without Bias (difficulty: 3)

---

### Module 4: Building the Business Model
**Duration:** 2–3 sessions

Lessons:
1. Business Model Canvas: Overview (difficulty: 3)
2. Value Proposition: Why Would Someone Pay For This? (difficulty: 3)
3. Revenue Models: Subscription, One-Time, Freemium (difficulty: 2)
4. Customer Segments (difficulty: 2)
5. Cost Structure Basics (difficulty: 2)
6. Channels: How Do You Reach Customers? (difficulty: 2)
7. Competition: Finding & Differentiating (difficulty: 3)

---

### Module 5: Pitching & Storytelling
**Duration:** 2–3 sessions

Lessons:
1. The Pitch Structure: Problem → Solution → Why Us → Ask (difficulty: 3)
2. Storytelling: Starting with the Problem, Not the Product (difficulty: 3)
3. Slide Deck Principles (difficulty: 2)
4. Demo: Showing vs. Telling (difficulty: 2)
5. Handling Questions (difficulty: 3)
6. The Elevator Pitch (60-second version) (difficulty: 3)
7. Practice Pitch & Feedback (difficulty: 3)

---

### Module 6: Youth Competitions & Programs
**Duration:** 1–2 sessions

Lessons:
1. DECA Overview & Competition Types (difficulty: 2)
2. Future Business Leaders of America (FBLA) (difficulty: 2)
3. FIRST Robotics Business Plan (difficulty: 3)
4. Diamond Challenge (student startup competition) (difficulty: 3)
5. Science Fair with Entrepreneurship Track (difficulty: 3)
6. Applying to Summer Programs: NFTE, Y Combinator, etc. (difficulty: 2)

## MetaMinds Connection

This subject is deeply connected to the MetaMinds mission:

- **Junior Mentors are entrepreneurs** — They manage student relationships, deliver sessions, and earn credits for their service
- **The mentor pipeline is a business model** — Students who understand entrepreneurship understand why MetaMinds is built this way
- **Future product ideas** — Some of the best feature ideas for MetaMinds may come from student entrepreneurs in this program

Tutors teaching Entrepreneurship should emphasize the connection to real MetaMinds experiences.

---

## Common Student Struggles

1. **Falling in love with their idea** — Refusing to pivot when validation shows no demand
2. **Building before validating** — Students want to code before they know if anyone wants the product
3. **Pitch over-explaining** — Students describe features instead of benefits
4. **Idea vs. execution** — Students think the idea is the hard part; it's the execution
5. **Fear of customer interviews** — Talking to strangers is uncomfortable; it needs practice
