Trigonometry bridges Geometry and Calculus. Students learn to work with angles beyond 90°, understand the unit circle, and analyze periodic functions. ACT Math tests Trig; SAT does not, but Pre-Calculus and AP Calculus require it. Trig is often rushed in standard math sequences — MetaMinds treats it as a full course.

## ACT vs. SAT Coverage

- **ACT Math** — Includes Trig (4–6 questions); SOHCAHTOA, unit circle, basic identities
- **SAT Math** — Does NOT include Trig directly (but radians appear in some problems)
- **AP Calculus** — Requires solid Trig fluency, especially trig identities and inverse functions
- **AP Physics** — Requires SOHCAHTOA and vectors

---

## Common Student Struggles

1. **Radians feel unnatural** — Students want to convert everything to degrees; resist this
2. **Unit circle under pressure** — Students who memorized vs. derived forget it on tests
3. **The ambiguous case (Law of Sines)** — Determining 0, 1, or 2 possible triangles is confusing
4. **Proving identities** — Students try to move things across the = sign (not allowed)
5. **Phase shift sign** — The shift for `sin(x - c)` moves right (+c), which feels backwards
6. **Inverse trig domain restrictions** — `arcsin` only returns values in [-π/2, π/2]
